[1]Welcome

Welcome, Commander. The Home Planet Station is where your fleet lives between runs of FTL: Faster Than Light. FTL keeps only the ship you are flying. The station keeps everything else: every ship you have docked, the crew and cargo you have set aside, and a record of all your fleet has done.

From the station you can board any ship in your fleet and fly her in FTL, move crew and gear between ships, buy from the stores your ships have found, repair and upgrade them, commission new ships, send spare crew on expeditions and trade with other commanders. The Federation Home Planet oversees it all, and in some modes will write to you directly.

One word needs care. In FTL, and on the station's screens, "a station" written with a small s means a beacon with a store, where a ship can trade. Your own station is always The Home Planet Station.

Most of what you do at the station can be done with FTL open. Anything that involves the ship you are flying, the one under ABOARD, needs FTL closed or sitting at its main menu. A few things that affect the whole fleet, such as sending your ships to FTL with Slipstream or switching game modes, need FTL closed altogether. The station will tell you whenever that matters.

Top

[2]Getting Started

What you need

You need FTL: Faster Than Light 1.6 (Steam, GOG or Humble) on Windows, and Java 8 or newer. If Java is missing, the station offers to fetch it for you. Slipstream Mod Manager is optional. It is only needed to fly ships you have retrofitted, remodeled or designed yourself, and the station can download it for you as well.

Before the first start

Close FTL before the station's first start. The station looks at FTL's saves while it sets up, and it is best if FTL isn't writing to them at the same time.

If you have runs or unlocks in FTL that you care about, make a copy of FTL's saves folder first, just in case. Where to find it is explained below.

If you are in the middle of a run in FTL and choose Sandbox Mode, that ship becomes the first ship in your fleet, with her crew, weapons, drones and cargo. If you have no run in progress, your fleet starts empty and you can commission your first ship from the Space Dock (section [6]). If you begin an Immersive career instead, a run in progress is kept safe in your Sandbox fleet, and the career starts afresh with an empty shipyard, a Kestrel to commission free of charge and a little scrap.

Starting the station

Unzip the station's folder somewhere you can easily find it, such as your Desktop or Documents. Avoid Program Files, because the station keeps a small settings file beside itself and Windows may not let it write there. Then double-click "Federation Home Planet Interface.bat". Beside it is a Documents folder holding this manual in text and with pictures, the list of changes, and the README, so you can read them without starting the station.

Windows may warn you the first time, because the file was downloaded from the internet. If it shows "Windows protected your PC", click More info and then Run anyway.

If no Java can be found, a small window offers three choices. Press 1 to download Java just for the station, into a folder named "java" beside it. Nothing is installed, and deleting that folder removes it again. Press 2 to download and install Java for the whole computer, in which case Windows will ask for your permission. Press 0 to cancel. If a download is interrupted, it picks up where it left off the next time you start the station.

If you would rather install Java yourself, the station uses Eclipse Temurin, which you can download free from adoptium.net.

The first start

The first time the station opens, it asks a few questions, one window at a time.

1

Where FTL keeps its saves. The station usually finds this folder by itself and only asks you to confirm it. If it can't, choose FTL's continue.sav or ae_prof.sav file and the station takes the folder from there. FTL keeps its saves in your Documents folder, under My Games:

Documents\My Games\FasterThanLight
2

Where FTL is installed. Confirm the folder the station found, or choose FTL's ftl.dat file, which sits in FTL's own folder. These are the usual places:

Steam
  C:\Program Files (x86)\Steam\steamapps\common\FTL Faster Than Light
GOG
  C:\Program Files (x86)\GOG.com\Faster Than Light
Humble
  C:\Program Files (x86)\FTL

If yours isn't there, FTL can show you. In Steam, right-click FTL in your library and choose Manage, then Browse local files. In GOG Galaxy, open FTL's page and choose Manage installation, then Show folder.

3

For the Steam version only, whether to launch FTL through Steam. After that comes a reminder to turn off Steam Cloud for FTL, which you should do. In your Steam library, right-click FTL, choose Properties, and on the General page untick the option to keep game saves in the Steam Cloud. With Steam Cloud on, Steam can bring back old copies of ships that are already docked at the station.

4

Slipstream Mod Manager. If you have it already, choose Browse... and show the station where it is. To have the station fetch it, choose Download it for me. Otherwise choose Not now. Everything except retrofitted, remodeled and designed ships works without it, and you can set it up later. If you would rather download it yourself, it is on its official page, under Files, Slipstream, 1.9.1:

https://sourceforge.net/projects/slipstreammodmanager/

Unzip it anywhere, then show the station where it is in SETTINGS, on the Folders tab, with the Change button beside Slipstream folder.

5

How you would like to serve. Sandbox Mode is FTL as you know it, with a station behind it and rules you choose for yourself. Immersive Mode is a Federation career with a fleet of its own and rules set by its difficulty. Both are explained in section [11]. If you are new to the station, Sandbox Mode is the place to start. If you choose it, a window of house rules opens next so you can decide how strict the station should be. Leave the ticks as they are if you aren't sure. The same rules can be changed at any time in SETTINGS, on the Rules tab.

Later, in SETTINGS, you can switch between Sandbox Mode and your careers, change the folders on the Folders tab, and change many other options. In an Immersive career, most of the rules are set by its difficulty and can't be changed (section [11]).

Where your fleet is kept

Your fleets and the station's settings are kept in FTL's saves folder, mostly in the folders whose names begin with FederationHomePlanet. Only a small file noting where things are, and the program's own logs, stay beside the program. A newer version of the station can be unzipped anywhere and will find everything without asking again. To back up your fleet, back up FTL's saves folder.

[3]Your First Voyage

When the station opens, you are on the Space Dock. The ship you last flew in FTL is shown at the top left under the heading ABOARD.
She is the ship FTL will load when you continue.

1

Click LAUNCH FTL, the first button in the column down the right side of the screen. FTL starts, and Continue on FTL's main menu picks up her run where you left it.

2

Play as you normally would. While you play, you can switch over to the station at any time, for example with Alt+Tab, to read transmissions, send other crew on expeditions, or do anything else that doesn't involve the ship you are flying. To change ships, or to do anything with her, save and quit to FTL's main menu, or close FTL. If you play FTL docked inside the station's own window instead, see section [10].

3

Back at the station, the Space Dock shows where your ship has been and what she carries now. The station takes stock by itself whenever you come back to it, so you will rarely need to do anything. If something ever looks out of date, click the refresh button, a small icon of two round arrows in the top right corner, just left of the gold HELM heading.

4

To fly a different ship, click BOARD under her picture in the list under the DOCKED heading. The ship you were flying is docked for you, and the one you chose moves up to ABOARD. Click LAUNCH FTL and continue as before.

A ship is only ever in one place. While she is aboard, FTL flies her. While she is docked, the station keeps her safe exactly as you left her, with her crew, weapons, drones and cargo, until you need her again.

When a run ends

A run in FTL ends in one of two ways, and the station handles each one differently.

If your ship is destroyed, her run ends as it always does, and the station records her as lost in action. She is remembered in the Memorial of the Federation Museum (section [12]). In Sandbox Mode she can be brought back from the station's last record of her, with Recover a ship under OTHER... (section [6]). In an Immersive career, a lost ship stays lost, except one lost to an accidental New Game (see below).

If your ship wins the final battle, what happens next depends on your rules. She may be gone with her run as in FTL, she may come back so you can choose between flying her again and giving her to the Federation Museum for a reward, or she may go straight to the museum. In Sandbox Mode it starts on Nothing, as in FTL, and in a career the difficulty decides. Either way the station must be open while you play. Section [12] explains it in full.

Take care not to start a New Game in FTL while a ship you want to keep is aboard. FTL writes the new game over her, and the station has to record her as lost. In Sandbox Mode the new run simply becomes the ship aboard, and the old one can be brought back with Recover a ship. In an Immersive career the new run is a ship the Federation never commissioned, and the station asks what to do with her (section [15]), while the old one may be offered back to you once. To start a fresh run, dock your ship first and commission a new one, or use NEW JOURNEY (section [4]).

[4]The Space Dock

The Space Dock is the station's main screen, and every other part of the station is reached from here.

The left side: your ships

At the top left, under the heading ABOARD, is the ship you are flying, with her class, sector, crew, hull, scrap, fuel, missiles and drone parts listed beside her picture. Under the picture are two buttons. DOCK puts her away in the dock until she is needed again. INFO opens her report, where you can read her history, see her stats, look through her records, or give her a new name with Rename. Clicking her picture does the same as INFO.

Below that, under the heading DOCKED, is every other ship in your fleet, each with BOARD and INFO buttons under her picture. A ship that is not at a beacon with a store shows "Not within range of a station" in orange. Some things, such as trading her cargo, can only be done once she reaches a store.

The right side: the station's buttons

Down the right side of the screen is a column of buttons in three groups, each under a gold heading.

Under HELM are LAUNCH FTL and NEW JOURNEY. LAUNCH FTL starts FTL with the ship you are aboard. While FTL is already running, it reads SWITCH TO FTL and simply brings FTL to the front. NEW JOURNEY sends the ship you are aboard back to the first sector for a fresh run, and her crew, cargo and supplies stay with her. Her star charts are wiped and you choose the difficulty for the new run. Your rules may ask that she be at a beacon with a store first, and may charge for a new journey. The fee is paid from the Cargo Hold, or with reputation if your reputation can be spent. In an Immersive career both of these always apply, with the fee set by the difficulty.

Under STATION are CARGO BAY (section [5]), LONG RANGE (section [9]), EXPEDITIONS (section [7]), QUARTERS (section [8]) and SETTINGS (section [13]). Once any ship of yours has won the final battle or been lost in action, a MUSEUM button joins the end of this group.

Under SHIPYARD are COMMISSION, DESIGN SHIP, JUNKYARD, DECOMMISSION and OTHER..., all described in section [6].

In the top right corner, just left of the HELM heading, is the refresh button, a small icon of two round arrows. The station takes stock of your fleet by itself whenever you come back to it, so it is rarely needed. Click it if the Space Dock ever looks out of date, such as after changing save files yourself.

The inbox and your reputation

When transmissions from The Federation Home Planet are turned on (they always are in an Immersive career), a small antenna icon appears at the right end of the ABOARD heading. If no ship is aboard, it sits at the right end of the DOCKED heading instead. When something new has arrived, its light turns green, the number of new transmissions shows beside it, and the icon gives a little hop now and then. Click it to open your Transmissions, where you read and answer letters and claim rewards, orders and shipments.

Reputation is your standing with The Federation Home Planet. Your fleet earns it through good service and loses it through losses. When reputation is in use, it shows in gold just right of the inbox, as REP followed by a number. Hover over it to see the latest changes, or click it to open the Career Reputation Log, which lists every gain and loss and explains how reputation is earned and spent. It has a Search box too.

[5]The Cargo Bay

Click CARGO BAY on the Space Dock to move crew, gear and supplies between your ships, buy from stores, and refit your ships.

RETURN TO DOCK is at the top left of the Cargo Bay. The tabs TRADE, SHOP and REFIT are in the middle of the top row, with RESET and SAVE at the right. Nothing you do in the Cargo Bay is final until you click SAVE. A gold dot on the SAVE button means there are changes not yet saved. RESET throws them away and puts everything back as it was. If you try to leave with changes not yet saved, the station asks whether to save them first.

The Trade tab

The Trade tab shows two ships side by side. On the left is the ship you are working on, named in the list under CURRENTLY ABOARD. You can pick any other docked ship from that list too, without boarding her. On the right, named in the list under TRADING WITH, is where her goods go. That is usually the Cargo Hold, but any other of your ships waiting at a store can be chosen from the same list.

Each side is divided into Supplies, Weapons, Drones, Augments and Crew. To move something, click it in either list and then use the buttons in the middle between the two lists. SEND moves the selected item from left to right, and TAKE moves it from right to left. Beside them are three small round buttons. The one marked with an i shows the item's details, the bin junks it for nothing, and the cog sells it at a store for half its price.

Crew are moved the same way. A crew member can also be sent into retirement with RETIRE, which means they leave your service for good, so the station asks you to confirm it first. Double-click anyone to see their skills and record, or to give them a new name.

To move supplies, click a supply box such as Fuel on either side. The middle then shows a number box with an arrow on each side. Set how many to move, then click the arrow that points the way they should go. The double arrows below move all of it at once.

Each ship has a limited number of weapon and drone slots, three augment slots and room for eight crew. When her weapon or drone slots are full, the station offers to put the item in her cargo in FTL, which has room for four items. When all three augment slots are full, send one of her augments away first.

The Cargo Bay works on a ship that is at a beacon with a store. A ship that hasn't yet left the place where the station set her out, such as one just commissioned or sent on a new journey, counts as being at one too. If she isn't, the Cargo Bay still opens, on the Trade tab with the Cargo Hold on the right, so you can look over what it holds, sell it or junk it. Under the list of ships it says why she can't trade, and while no ship in range is chosen, the Shop and Refit tabs stay greyed out. Pick another ship that is at a store, or fly one to a store in FTL first. In Sandbox Mode you can lift this rule instead, by unticking "Trading and scrapping need a station" in SETTINGS on the Rules tab. That opens the Trade and Refit tabs anywhere, though the Shop still only sells from real stores. In an Immersive career the rule always applies.

The Cargo Hold

The Cargo Hold is the station's own storage, and it has no limit on slots. Gear, supplies, systems and crew can all be kept there for as long as you like. Crew waiting in the Cargo Hold are the ones you can send on expeditions, and what your expeditions bring home ends up there (section [7]).

The Shop tab

The Shop shows the stores at your ships' beacons. Every ship of yours that is sitting at a beacon with a store right now brings that store's stock to the station, so the more of your ships are at stores, the more you can buy.

At the top of the tab, BUYING FOR chooses whether the goods go to the ship you are working on or to the Cargo Hold. Beside it, PAY FROM chooses whether the scrap comes from the ship or from the Cargo Hold. A Buy button that is greyed out can't be used right now. Hover over it to see why. As with everything in the Cargo Bay, nothing is paid until you click SAVE.

The Refit tab

The Refit tab shows your ship's deck plan with two lists beside it. Installed systems lists what she has fitted now, and Uninstalled systems in the Cargo Bay lists the systems kept in storage at the station. Choose a system and click Uninstall to take it off her and keep it in storage, or Install to fit a stored one. Taking a system off may cost a fee. In Sandbox Mode it is free unless you choose a fee or forbid it on the Rules tab. In an Immersive career each system taken off costs scrap or reputation, set by the difficulty. Stored systems can be sold if your rules allow it, and in a career they always can be. The Refit tab has its own PAY FROM switch for these costs.

The systems a ship is built with, her standard equipment, can't come off until she has been retrofitted, so their Uninstall buttons are greyed out. Hover over one to see why. The Retrofit button here does that, and it needs the Federation Home Planet Mod to be in FTL first (section [14]). FTL may then stop counting her as her original ship for its achievements, and the station warns you before you retrofit. A retrofitted ship can also be remodeled, which in an Immersive career needs the rank of Commander.

Repairs and upgrades

Repairs and upgrades are made on the Refit tab, in the first rows of the Installed systems list, with UP and other buttons beside them. The Hull row repairs one point at a time or all at once, and a Hull breaches row seals any breaches. The Reactor row adds reactor power. Each system has a button to upgrade it, or to fix it first if it is damaged. Every button shows its price.

Repairs and upgrades are paid by whichever side the Refit tab's PAY FROM switch names, when you click SAVE.

The Power Distribution Simulation shows how her reactor power would spread across her systems. Left-click a system to add power and right-click to take it away, just as in FTL. You can also drag weapons and drones between their slots here, and the new order is kept when you click SAVE. The power itself is only a trial, and FTL keeps its own.

[6]The Shipyard

Commission

Click COMMISSION to have The Federation Home Planet build you a new ship. The list on the left shows the Special Federation Ships, such as the Relief Ship, then every standard ship and layout you can have, and any ships of your own design under Your blueprints. Ships still locked in your FTL profile may be hidden, if your rules say so. In an Immersive career they always are.

Choose a ship on the left to see her on the right, with her hull, reactor, weapons, crew and price. Type her a name, or click the die beside the name for a new one. To the right of the name, choose her difficulty and her content, Original or AE (FTL's Advanced Edition). Click a crew member's name to change it, or the die beside the Crew heading to give them all new names. When you are ready, click Commission. She is built to FTL's starting specifications and waits at the Space Dock, and unless FTL is running, the station offers to board her straight away.

In Sandbox Mode commissioning is free, unless you tick "Commissioning a ship costs scrap" in SETTINGS on the Rules tab. In an Immersive career it always costs scrap, at a share of her value set by the difficulty. Your career's first ship is free, though, and so is each ship layout you newly unlock in FTL, once. The price is shown before you commit, and it is paid from the Cargo Hold. In a career, ships of your own design are listed but can only be commissioned from the rank of Commodore (section [11]).

A ship marked "not in FTL yet" can still be commissioned, but she can't fly until her blueprint has been sent to FTL. See section [14].

Design Ship

DESIGN SHIP opens a list of your designs. Click New design to start from an empty grid, or From a game ship to start from one of FTL's own. The designer works in steps, shown as tabs along its top, from her rooms and doors to her look and her loadout, and Help explains each one. Save design keeps your work to finish later. On the Loadout step, tick "Starter ship (available to commission)" so that she appears in COMMISSION under Your blueprints, then click Build blueprint. She is sent to FTL through Slipstream (section [14]). In an Immersive career, DESIGN SHIP opens from the rank of Commodore.

The Junkyard

Click JUNKYARD to see the ships you have put out of service. Choose one from the list to decide her fate.

Salvage brings her back to the Space Dock with her crew and cargo as they were. Scrap breaks her up, so the hull and her standard systems are gone for good but everything else she carries goes to the Cargo Hold. Your rules may also let you strip her other systems for a fee. Trade In sells her to The Federation Home Planet for part of her value, and Auction sells her to whichever bidder offers the most, which may be more or less than a trade-in. A sale keeps only her scrap and crew, which come to the Cargo Hold with the payment. Everything else she carries goes with her.

Scrap, Trade In and Auction need her to be at a beacon with a store, just as trading does. If she isn't, salvage her and fly her to one first. In Sandbox Mode this rule can be turned off (section [5]), and in an Immersive career it always applies.

Destroy removes her and everything she carries with nothing gained, so the station asks you to confirm it first. In Sandbox Mode a destroyed ship can still be brought back later with Recover a ship. In an Immersive career it is final, but the station also offers to send her to your Sandbox fleet instead.

The Junkyard's foreman also sells hulls and damaged parts. Derelicts lists old hulls you can buy. A derelict is delivered to the Junkyard, where you salvage her to fly her. She comes without crew, and is often missing a system such as Engines, Piloting or Oxygen. Parts lists damaged systems, which go into storage and can be repaired on the Refit tab once installed. Each part has an Info button and a hover that say what it is. New stock arrives as your fleet travels, and once a week you can click Search for more parts, which costs 15 scrap from the Cargo Hold and takes the day. It finds parts and salvage, never derelicts. Buying anything from the Junkyard takes the day as well.

Decommission

DECOMMISSION retires the ship you are aboard. Her crew stand down, and she goes to the Junkyard with everything she carries. If you salvage her later, her crew come back with her.

Other orders

OTHER... opens a list of less common orders, each with a short explanation beside it. An order that can't be used right now is greyed out, with the reason shown in orange.

One of them, Recover a ship, brings back a ship lost to a malfunction or lost in action, from the station's last record of her. It only works in Sandbox Mode. In an Immersive career it is shown greyed out, because there lost ships stay lost.

Another, Plead for New Ship, asks The Federation Home Planet for a ship. It is offered whenever commissioning costs scrap. Which ship is granted depends on your rules. In Sandbox Mode it is set by "Plead for New Ship grants" on the Rules tab. In a career, Easy grants any ship, Normal a Kestrel Type A or the Relief Ship, and Hard only the Relief Ship Type A. The ship granted waits for you in COMMISSION. You pay for her there by giving up the Cargo Hold's contents. Its crew stay, and any value left over is offered back. If your reputation can be spent, you can keep the hold and pay in reputation instead. Withdraw Plea, in the same list, cancels a plea that is still waiting.

[7]Expeditions

Crew without a ship needn't sit idle. Click EXPEDITIONS on the Space Dock to send them out on jobs.

The Expeditions window has two tabs. Choose an Offer shows three offers side by side, each from a different sector. Current expeditions shows who is out, team by team, with the sector and the day they left, but never when they are due back. To send a party, click Send crew under the offer you like and choose up to three crew from the Cargo Hold. If nobody is waiting in the Cargo Hold, move some crew there first in the Cargo Bay, or post for volunteers.

The party is away for a while, usually a week or two. Time at the station passes as your ships travel in FTL, and when you rest in your quarters (section [8]). When the party returns, their report arrives, telling what the job was and how each of them fared. Each crew member's line is marked with one to three gold stars for a success, the more the better, or a cross for a failure. Lines for anyone injured, killed or captured carry no mark, since the report says what befell them.

A party brings home scrap, and sometimes an item one of them found along the way. When transmissions are turned on (always, in an Immersive career), these come with the party's report as a letter in your Transmissions, and you claim them from there. Otherwise they go straight into the Cargo Hold. Now and then a party brings home something bigger. A captured ship or a rescued survivor who would like to join you comes with a question about what to do with them, and a salvaged system goes straight into storage. A Rescue job that goes well brings home a survivor who wants to sign on fairly often. A Hijack or Salvage job now and then finds someone aboard who wishes to join. A really good Salvage or Hijack can also bring home a few missiles, some fuel or a few drone parts. Crew also come home a little more practised at their work, and when reputation is in use, how the job went earns your fleet reputation or costs it some. Expeditions are dangerous, though, and not everyone comes back.

When the infirmary is turned on (SETTINGS, Rules tab, under Expeditions), crew who come home badly hurt rest in the infirmary for a while, and each loses a level in one of their skills. A crewmate's outstanding success can also save someone who would otherwise have died, and the one saved goes to the infirmary too. Until they recover, crew in the infirmary can't be moved, traded or sent out again.

A crew member taken captive is held for ransom. A letter arrives in your Transmissions asking for payment, or a message on the Space Dock if transmissions are off. You have one month to pay it from the Cargo Hold. If you refuse, or the month runs out, they are lost for good.

At the bottom of the Expeditions window, Post for volunteers asks for new crew. It costs a little scrap from the Cargo Hold, more the larger your fleet's crew, and the scrap is spent whether or not anyone answers. Posting also takes the day. If anyone answers, they are hired on the next day and wait in the Cargo Hold. If your fleet has no crew left anywhere, the button offers a promise of adventure instead. It costs no scrap, though it costs some reputation whenever reputation is in use, and fewer people answer it.

[8]Captain's Quarters and the Records

Captain's Quarters

Click QUARTERS on the Space Dock to retire to your quarters, and choose Rest to stay there for the day. A day of rest lets time pass at the station without anyone flying, so crew can return from expeditions or leave the infirmary. The first day of rest is free. If reputation is in use, each further day in a row costs a little, since people begin to talk.

Captain's Log, also in your quarters, tells your career as a story, a day at a time. Tick Detailed Log Entries to add details such as costs and reputation, where a day has any.

The Records tab

The station's logs can be read in SETTINGS on the Records tab. View Station Log shows everything the station has done for your fleet, View Ship Log shows one ship's voyages (choose her from the list below the button), and View Crew Log shows every crew member who has served with you, where they served and what became of them. The station log and a ship's log each have a Search box, and the Crew Log does not. The Search ignores capitals, finds parts of words, and finds words in the order you type them, even across a line break. A ship's Records have the same Search on their log tabs.

[9]Long Range Comm.

Long Range Comm. lets you talk and trade with another commander's Home Planet Station. Click LONG RANGE on the Space Dock to open it. The first time, the station asks for your commander's name, which is how other commanders will know you. Your rank goes in front of it, and you can change the name later in SETTINGS on the General tab.

Connecting to another commander

Both of you need to do the following.

1

Click LONG RANGE on the Space Dock.

2

Click Open Hailing Frequencies. The first time, Windows may ask whether to let Java use the network. Allow it on private networks. The status line then reads HAILING FREQUENCIES OPEN and shows your hailing frequency, which is a port number.

3

If you are both on the same home network, you each appear in the other's Commanders list automatically. One of you chooses the other and clicks Hail. It doesn't matter which of you hails.

4

If you are not on the same network, use the By address box below the Commanders list. Type the other commander's address there, adding a colon and their port number if it isn't 47610, then click the Hail button beside the box. Players who share a virtual network such as Tailscale, Hamachi or ZeroTier need nothing else. To be hailed over the open internet, the commander being hailed must forward their port on their router and share their public address.

5

The other commander is asked whether to answer the hail. They can Answer, Decline or Block it.

Once the hail is answered, you are connected. Leaving the Long Range screen closes your hailing frequencies, unless you click Stay Powered Up first and confirm it. That keeps you reachable while you use the rest of the station, and the button then reads Power Down. Click it when you want to stop. The LONG RANGE button's lamp is green while you are powered up, and turns orange if you miss a hail.

Trading

Once connected, your goods are on the left and the other commander's are on the right. The OFFERING FROM list at the top left chooses where your side of the trade comes from, the Cargo Hold or one of your ships. Choose something and click Offer to add it to your side of the trade, or Take back to remove it. Offer all adds everything of one kind, and Offer the whole ship offers the ship herself.

A ship can only offer her goods while she is at a beacon with a store, or still where the station set her out, unless your Sandbox rules lift that (section [5]). To offer the ship herself, she must also be docked rather than under ABOARD. A ship offering her crew must keep at least one of them, and crew in the infirmary can't be offered. Only the other commander can add their own goods. When you are both content, each of you clicks Accept. Changing either offer withdraws both acceptances, so nobody can be surprised. Goods you receive arrive in the Cargo Hold, and a ship arrives docked at the Space Dock. There is a chat box below for talking things over, and Disconnect ends the call.

Supplies, weapons, drones, augments and crew can all be traded. Whole ships can be traded too. Sandbox fleets may always trade ships, but Immersive careers may only when both commanders allow it in SETTINGS on the General tab. A Sandbox fleet trades only with other Sandbox fleets, and an Immersive career only with other Immersive careers. Careers of different difficulties can trade too. This is allowed unless either commander turns it off. Otherwise you can still talk, but not trade.

Messages and shipments

You can also write to another commander without connecting. Choose them in the Commanders list and click Send Message, and your letter waits in their inbox. Tick Priority to have it pop up on their screen instead, if they allow priority messages to pop up. Prepare Shipment lets you pack items, supplies and crew to send along with a message. A message to a commander who can't be reached right now waits in your Outbox, and it is delivered once both of you have hailing frequencies open.

To stop a commander from hailing or writing to you, right-click them in the Commanders list and choose Block. Blocked commanders can be unblocked the same way, or in SETTINGS on the General tab.

[10]Playing FTL Docked

On Windows, FTL can be played inside the station's own window, with your inbox, reputation and the station's buttons still around it. This is still an experimental feature.

Turning it on

1

Click SETTINGS on the Space Dock and choose the General tab.

2

Under Launching, tick "Option to Play FTL, docked in the station window, at", then choose a size for FTL from the list beside it.

3

Click OK.

Launching docked

A small screen icon now appears in the top right corner of the Space Dock, directly under the refresh button and just left of LAUNCH FTL. Click it to start FTL docked. If FTL is already running in a window, the icon docks that window instead of starting a second FTL. FTL plays in a gold frame where your ship and the station were shown.

While FTL is docked, that icon becomes an X. Clicking the X asks whether to Remove from Dock, which puts FTL back in a window of its own, or to Close FTL. A small flip icon on the ABOARD heading switches between FTL and the Space Dock, so you can check on the station without closing the game. New transmissions pop up at the top right of FTL's frame.

If FTL was already running full screen, the station asks what you would like to do, since a full-screen FTL can't be docked. The simplest answer is to let it close FTL and start it again docked. Anything FTL hasn't saved yet may be lost when it closes.

While the docked option is on, FTL is set to play in a window, since that is how it docks. Your own full-screen setting is put back when you turn the option off. LAUNCH FTL still starts FTL in a window of its own, so if you want it full screen when launched that way, tick "Undocked launches: set FTL's Fullscreen to" under Launching in Settings and choose how. You can set vertical sync there too.

[11]Sandbox Mode and Immersive Careers

Sandbox Mode

Sandbox Mode is FTL as you know it, with a station behind it. Commission ships, trade freely between your ships and design your own. Every rule is yours to change in SETTINGS on the Rules tab, including whether you hold a rank and how it is earned. If you turn on Immersive Notifications and Career messages there, The Federation Home Planet writes to you in Sandbox Mode too, with promotions, rewards for FTL's achievements and a stipend.

Immersive Mode

Immersive Mode is a career with The Federation Home Planet. You begin with an empty shipyard, a Kestrel to commission free of charge and a little scrap. The Federation sets most of the rules, and they are locked while the career runs. Trading, scrapping and new journeys need a beacon with a store. Commissioning costs scrap, ships still locked in FTL can't be commissioned, and each ship you unlock in FTL from now on can be commissioned free, once. Lost ships stay lost, and can't be recovered. Transmissions, reputation and rank are always on. How your reputation can be spent and whether the infirmary is used stay your own choices.

Your rank rises with your reputation and is never taken away. You start as a Major. At 250 reputation you become a Colonel, at 500 a Commander, at 1,000 a Captain, at 2,500 a Commodore and at 5,000 an Admiral. A Commander may remodel ships. A Commodore may design ships and commission ships of their own design. An Admiral may fit the Federation's own artillery. Each Federation Cruiser layout you unlock in FTL also earns 100 reputation. Outside a career, nothing is locked by rank.

The Federation Home Planet writes to you with orders, promotions, rewards for FTL's achievements and a regular stipend. Rewards and the stipend come as letters in your Transmissions, and you claim each one there to have it paid into the Cargo Hold. An unclaimed stipend stays in your inbox until you claim it. The stipend grows with your rank and with the achievements it counts.

There are four careers, Easy, Normal, Hard and Custom. Each has its own fleet, kept apart from your Sandbox fleet and from each other.

Easy     Ships cost half their value. A new journey costs 200 scrap.
         A plea can grant any ship. The stipend comes every month.
         The career starts with 50 scrap.
Normal   Ships cost three quarters of their value. A new journey
         costs 500 scrap. The stipend comes every two months.
         The career starts with 25 scrap.
Hard     Ships cost their full value. A new journey costs 1,000
         scrap. The stipend comes every three months. The career
         starts with 10 scrap. An augment that finds no room is lost,
         but reputation is earned twice as fast and rank climbs
         quickest.
Custom   You choose each rule yourself, once, when the career begins.

The briefing lists every rule for each.

To begin a career, choose one when the station first starts, or in SETTINGS on the Rules tab click Switch Game Mode and choose Begin beside the career you want. A briefing of three pages explains what changes. On its second page you can give the career an FTL profile of its own (recommended), which starts FTL fresh with only the Kestrel unlocked. Your own profile is set aside and comes back when you return to Sandbox Mode. Read the briefing through and click Confirm on the last page to begin.

A career's difficulty can't be changed once it has begun. To start one again, choose End career... beside it in Switch Game Mode. The station asks twice and keeps a copy of the old career. Then it removes the career with its ships, Cargo Hold, rank, transmissions and FTL profile. Beginning that career again then starts a new one.

Switching modes

You can move between Sandbox Mode and your careers at any time. In SETTINGS, on the Rules tab, click Switch Game Mode and choose Switch beside the mode you want. FTL must be closed altogether, not just at its menu. Switching to Sandbox Mode asks you to confirm it. Switching into a career shows its briefing again, so you can check its choices before clicking Confirm, and going from one career to another passes through Sandbox Mode on the way. The ship you are aboard is docked before you go, and she is boarded again when you come back.

[12]After a Final Victory

In FTL, a ship that wins the final battle is lost with her run. The station can keep her instead, if you wish. In SETTINGS, on the Rules tab, under After a final victory, there are three choices.

With "Nothing", the station does as FTL does, and the ship is gone when her run ends. With "Choice", she comes back as she was just before the final battle, and you decide whether to keep flying her or give her a place in the Federation Museum for a reward. The question arrives as a letter in your Transmissions, or on the Space Dock if transmissions are off. A ship you keep is docked, ready to set out on a new journey from the first sector. With "Museum", she goes straight to the museum, and the reward is paid into the Cargo Hold.

In an Immersive career, the difficulty decides. Easy and Normal give you the choice, and Hard always sends her to the museum. The museum pays her full value on Easy and half on Normal and Hard. On Normal a ship you keep always flies on at Hard, and on Easy you choose. Custom does whatever you picked when the career began.

For "Choice" or "Museum", the station must be open while you play, so that it can keep her as she was before the last battle. If it isn't, she is lost with her run as in FTL. In Sandbox Mode, below the three choices, Museum reward value sets what the museum pays for her, and Rescued Ships after Victory moved to Hard difficulty decides whether a ship you keep flies on at Hard.

The museum is reached with the MUSEUM button on the Space Dock. Its Hall of Victors honours the ships that won, and its Memorial remembers those lost in action.

[13]Settings

Click SETTINGS on the Space Dock to open the Settings window. It has six tabs along the top, General, Rules, Folders, Mods, Records and About. Most changes take effect when you click OK at the bottom right, and Cancel closes the window without them. A few buttons act straight away and aren't undone by Cancel: Switch Game Mode, End career, Patch mods, Blueprints, unblocking a commander, and changing the Slipstream folder.

General

Under Commander is your name for Long Range Comm. Under Launching you choose whether to launch FTL through Steam. On Windows you also find the options for playing FTL docked (section [10]) and for launching FTL with DirectX, which may help it load faster. The last option there sets whether FTL starts full screen when launched on its own.

Under Station, Borderless full screen lets the station fill your screen with no title bar. F11 or Alt+Enter switches it at any time. You can also turn the title music on or off, and choose whether the station speaks of a ship as her, him or it.

Under Crew Names are choices for how new crew are named, with name lists for each race. Under Long Range Comm. are the trading choices for Immersive careers, whether priority messages pop up, and the list of commanders you have blocked.

Rules

The Rules tab holds the rules of the mode you are in. At the top, Switch Game Mode moves between Sandbox Mode and your careers. In an Immersive career, most rules are set by its difficulty and shown greyed out, marked "Set by Immersive Mode". In Sandbox Mode, hover over any rule to read what it does.

The rules are grouped by subject. Under The Federation Home Planet you decide whether transmissions and reputation are in use, how reputation can be spent, and how ranks are earned. Journeys and trading covers whether trading and new journeys need a store, and what a new journey costs. Refit, scrapping and selling covers the fees for taking systems off and what may be sold. Shipyard covers which ships can be commissioned and at what price, and Ship and part values sets how much ships and parts are worth wherever the station prices them: Trade In, Auction, the museum and the Junkyard. In a career it is set by the difficulty. Expeditions holds the infirmary (section [7]), and After a final victory is explained in section [12].

Folders

The Folders tab shows where FTL's saves, FTL itself and Slipstream are kept, each with a Change button. Below them are buttons that open the station's folders on your computer, such as the Cargo Hold or the station's logs, in case you ever need to look inside.

Mods

The Mods tab sends your own ships to FTL (section [14]). Blueprints lets you choose which of your blueprints are starter ships offered in COMMISSION, and change their class and name.

Records

The Records tab shows the station's logs (section [8]). The list under View Station Log chooses whose log to read, your Sandbox fleet's or a career's. Below them, Send Feedback opens a form where you can report a problem or suggest an idea, and Debug Log Folder opens the program's own logs, which are useful to send along with a report. Tick Debug logging only if you are asked to, since it adds extra detail to those logs.

About

The About tab shows the station's version, the credits and the licence, with buttons to Check for Updates and Send Feedback. Under User Manual are two buttons, Html and Text, that open this manual: Html with its pictures, and Text as plain words. Beside them, "Open in The Home Planet Station" chooses where it opens. On, which is usual, shows it in a window of the station's own, where both manuals have a Find box (Ctrl+F) and the Html manual has a dark mode that is remembered. Off opens it in your web browser or text editor instead.

[14]Slipstream and Your Own Ships

Ships you retrofit, remodel or design yourself need their blueprints sent to FTL before they can fly. The station packs them into its own mod, the Federation Home Planet Mod, and sends it to FTL via Slipstream Mod Manager.

To remodel a ship, open the Refit tab in the Cargo Bay. She must be retrofitted first with the Retrofit button there, and then Remodel lets you move her systems and doors or rework her deck plan. Designing a ship from scratch is done with DESIGN SHIP on the Space Dock (section [6]).

When you finish a remodel or a design, the station offers to send it to FTL straight away. You can also do it at any time in SETTINGS, on the Mods tab, by clicking Patch mods. FTL must be closed while this is done. Afterwards the station offers to restart so it can see your new ships. The next time FTL starts, your ships are there.

Other FTL mods can be sent along too. Drop the mod's .ftl file into Slipstream's mods folder (Open mods folder on the same tab finds it), and tick it in the patch window. Each patch starts from a clean copy of FTL, so any mod left unticked is removed. The Federation Home Planet Mod is always included.

[15]When Something Goes Wrong

"Return to The Station to do this. (Close FTL)"

Anything that involves the ship you are flying, the one under ABOARD, waits while FTL is flying her, because FTL could write over the change. So do a few things that affect the whole fleet, such as Patch mods and switching game modes. For the ship you are flying, save and quit to FTL's main menu, or close FTL, and try again. For the fleet-wide actions, close FTL altogether. If the station warns that FTL is running when you board or dock a ship, choose Nevermind if you are still playing, or Go ahead if FTL is only sitting at its menu.

FTL opened, but my ship isn't there

Make sure she is the ship under ABOARD on the Space Dock before you click LAUNCH FTL, then choose Continue on FTL's main menu. If you remodeled or designed her, her blueprint must be sent to FTL first (section [14]).

A ship is marked "not in FTL yet"

Her blueprint hasn't been sent to FTL. The same thing shows as "(not patched in)" beside her layout on the Refit tab. In SETTINGS, on the Mods tab, click Patch mods with FTL closed, then let the station restart.

The Space Dock doesn't show what happened in FTL

Click the refresh button, the small icon of two round arrows in the top right corner of the Space Dock, and the station takes stock again.

My ship was destroyed, or I started a New Game by mistake

The station records her as lost in action. In Sandbox Mode, OTHER... on the Space Dock offers Recover a ship, which brings her back from the station's last record of her (section [6]). In an Immersive career a lost ship stays lost, although one lost to an accidental New Game may be offered back once.

"Steam Cloud brought back an old copy"

Steam Cloud restored an old save of a ship that is already in your fleet. The station set the copy aside rather than adding a second ship. Turn off Steam Cloud for FTL to stop it happening (section [2]).

"Uncommissioned ship detected"

In an Immersive career, the station found a ship it didn't commission, usually one flown in Sandbox Mode or begun in FTL directly. It asks what to do with her. You can send her to your Sandbox fleet's Space Dock, decommission her (to the Sandbox fleet's Junkyard, or destroyed), switch to Sandbox Mode now, or close the station. If you close the window without choosing, it asks again next time.

I can't trade a ship's cargo

She needs to be at a beacon with a store, or still where the station set her out. Fly her to one in FTL, or choose another ship in the Cargo Bay. In Sandbox Mode this rule can be turned off in SETTINGS on the Rules tab.

A button is greyed out

Hover over it. The station almost always says why it can't be used right now. In an Immersive career, a greyed-out rule in Settings simply says "Set by Immersive Mode".

Long Range Comm. can't find the other commander

Check that both of you clicked Open Hailing Frequencies and that Windows allowed Java on the network. If you aren't on the same home network, use the By address box (section [9]).

Reporting a problem

In SETTINGS, on the Records tab, click Send Feedback and describe what happened. If you are asked for the program's logs, Debug Log Folder on the same tab opens them.

[16]Federation Glossary

These are the words this manual and the station use for particular things, in alphabetical order. A word in a definition that has an entry of its own can be looked up here too.

Aboard is the ship you are flying, the one FTL loads when you continue. Only one ship is aboard at a time.

An augment is a piece of gear that gives a ship a lasting benefit. A ship has room for three.

To board a ship is to take command of her, so that FTL flies her next. The ship that was aboard is docked.

The Cargo Bay is where you trade, shop and refit. The Cargo Hold is the station's own storage, with no limit on slots, for gear, supplies, systems and crew. It is not a ship.

To commission a ship is to have The Federation Home Planet build her for you. She is built to FTL's starting specifications and waits at the Space Dock.

A Custom career is an Immersive career whose rules you choose yourself, once, when it begins.

To decommission a ship is to take her out of service and send her to the Junkyard, with everything she carries.

A derelict is an old hull for sale in the Junkyard. She comes without crew and is often missing a system.

Docked describes a ship kept safe at the station, ready to be boarded.

Expeditions are jobs for crew who have no ship. A party goes out for a while and comes home with a report.

The Federation Home Planet is the authority you serve. It approves, commissions and writes to you. The Home Planet Station is the station that keeps your fleet, and the one you are using now.

The Federation Home Planet Mod is the station's own mod for FTL. It carries the blueprints of ships you retrofit, remodel or design, and it is sent to FTL with Slipstream, the mod manager the station uses.

Hailing frequencies are the channels you open in Long Range Comm. so that another commander's station can find yours, and the two can talk or trade.

An Immersive career is a career with The Federation Home Planet, with rules set by its difficulty. It can keep an FTL profile of its own. Sandbox Mode is FTL as you know it, with rules you choose yourself.

The infirmary is where crew hurt or saved from death on an expedition rest until they recover.

The Junkyard is where ships out of service wait. From there a ship can be salvaged back into service, scrapped, traded in or sold at auction.

The Federation Museum honours the ships that won the final battle in its Hall of Victors, and remembers those lost in action in its Memorial. Its button appears on the Space Dock once either has happened.

A new journey sends a ship back to the first sector for a fresh run. Her crew, cargo and supplies stay with her.

To patch is to send your mods and your ships' blueprints to FTL through Slipstream. FTL must be closed while it happens.

A plea, in full Plead for New Ship, asks The Federation Home Planet for a ship, which you then pay for in the Commission window.

Quarters are where you rest for the day and read the Captain's Log. They are reached with the QUARTERS button on the Space Dock.

Rank is your standing as a commander. In a career it climbs from Major to Admiral as your reputation grows, and it is never taken away. Reputation is your standing with The Federation Home Planet.

The Refit tab is where a ship's systems are installed, removed, repaired and upgraded, in the Cargo Bay.

To remodel a ship is to move her systems into other rooms and rework her doors and deck plan. A ship must be retrofitted first.

To retire a crew member is to let them leave your service for good. The station asks you to confirm it first.

To retrofit a ship is to change her onto a blank copy of her model, with no system counted as standard equipment. Afterwards any system can be taken off, and a remodel can change which room each system sits in. A retrofitted ship needs the Federation Home Planet Mod in FTL.

Scrap is the money of FTL and of the station. A ship carries her own, and the Cargo Hold keeps its own.

A starter ship is a design you have marked so that she appears in COMMISSION under Your blueprints.

A station, written with a small s, is a beacon with a store in FTL. Your own station is The Home Planet Station.

The stipend is the regular payment from The Federation Home Planet, paid into the Cargo Hold as a letter you claim.

Transmissions are the letters, orders and rewards from The Federation Home Planet, kept in the inbox, whose icon sits at the top of the Space Dock.
